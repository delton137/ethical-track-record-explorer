## Dating

- **Deuteronomy: c.800–500 BCE.** Broad editorial envelope for legal roots through exilic revision, based on [Christine Hayes, Yale lecture 11](https://oyc.yale.edu/religious-studies/rlst-145/lecture-11). This is not a precise date for individual laws.
- **Leviticus: c.600–400 BCE.** Editorial envelope for exilic/post-exilic priestly and Holiness redaction; older traditions remain possible. [Hayes, Yale lecture 9](https://oyc.yale.edu/religious-studies/rlst-145/lecture-9).
- **Ephesians: c.60–100 CE.** Envelope preserving both early Pauline and later disciple hypotheses. [USCCB introduction](https://bible.usccb.org/bible/ephesians/0).
- **Galatians: c.54–55 CE.** North-Galatian dating; earlier alternatives acknowledged. [USCCB introduction](https://bible.usccb.org/bible/galatians/0).
- **John: c.90–100 CE.** Final editorial form. [USCCB introduction](https://bible.usccb.org/bible/john/0).
- **Revelation: c.90–96 CE.** Approximation to the common late-Domitian dating; disputed alternatives acknowledged. [USCCB introduction](https://bible.usccb.org/bible/revelation/0).
- **Quran: c.610–632 CE.** [Corpus Coranicum’s research description](https://corpuscoranicum.de/de/about/research) supplies the proclamation envelope. Per-surah information pages linked in the records establish the traditional Meccan/Medinan classification. Broad periods avoid invented exact verse dates. The public-only proxy is conventional written standardization under Uthman, **644–656 CE**, following [Cohen and Ekhtiar, Metropolitan Museum](https://www.metmuseum.org/essays/early-qurans-8thearly-13th-centuries). This is not a radiocarbon date, a verse-specific surviving manuscript date, or a claim that oral circulation began that late.

## Interpretation and display

Eight passages receive qualified comparisons to existing benchmarks and are plotted: five Bible points and three Quran points. The thirteen unmatched passages are retained in the evidence table only. Each text’s plotted points form a compact group with 12-pixel vertical spacing (48 pixels total for Bible, 24 for Quran), and hover labels are spaced separately. Horizontal dates and numerical scores are unchanged; connections follow date midpoints without asserting exact passage order.

Eternal life in John 3:16 and Quran 4:57 is conditional on belief (and good works in 4:57). Revelation 21 describes renewed creation and differentiated destinies. These support afterlife promises, not the stronger claim that every human will enter heaven or that biological humanity cannot go extinct on Earth. They are not coded as opposition to extinction prevention.

The child passages do not establish modern child-labor prohibitions. Animal community language does not by itself establish legal rights or anti-cruelty legislation. Permission to eat meat does not settle a position on factory-farming welfare. Quran 7:81’s moral condemnation is not assigned a human criminal penalty from hadith or later law. Explicit death sentences in Leviticus are distinguished from that issue.

This is a curated selection with counterpassages, not an exhaustive concordance, a completed historical audit, or a claim that either collection has one uniform ethical program.
